Responsible automation

Make AI useful, understandable, and accountable.

People should know when AI is involved and have a meaningful way to review its work.

AI & automation3 minute read

Be clear about what the system does

AI is most useful when people understand the role it plays in a workflow. Explain what information it uses, what kind of output it provides, and where its limitations matter. This helps users interpret suggestions as assistance rather than treating every generated answer as a verified fact.

Set expectations in the product itself. A clear label, an explanation of the source of a recommendation, or a visible uncertainty indicator can help people decide when to check an output more closely.

Keep people in control of important decisions

Not every task needs the same level of oversight. Sorting low-risk information may need a different review process from making decisions that affect a person's access, finances, or wellbeing. Identify who is accountable for a decision and how they can correct or override an automated suggestion.

Make those controls practical. People need enough time, context, and training to review a result rather than simply approving it by default.

Monitor outcomes after launch

AI systems and the situations around them change over time. Monitor whether the feature is helping users, where it produces errors, and whether some groups experience different outcomes. Provide an easy way to report problems and a clear process for responding.

Responsible AI is an ongoing product and operational commitment. Keep the system understandable, assign ownership, and be ready to adjust or pause it when evidence shows that it is not meeting its purpose.

Key takeaway

Explain AI's role, preserve human accountability for important outcomes, and monitor how the system performs in practice.
